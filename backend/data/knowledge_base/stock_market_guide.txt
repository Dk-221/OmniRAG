Stock Market Investing: A Comprehensive Guide for Beginners
===========================================================

Section 1: Understanding the Stock Market

The stock market is a marketplace where buyers and sellers trade shares of publicly
listed companies. Key concepts:

- Stock/Share: A unit of ownership in a company.
- Exchange: A regulated marketplace (NYSE, NASDAQ, BSE, NSE) where stocks are traded.
- Bull Market: A period of rising stock prices (typically >20% gain from recent low).
- Bear Market: A period of falling stock prices (typically >20% decline from recent high).
- Market Capitalization: Total market value of a company = Share Price × Shares Outstanding.

Major US Stock Exchanges:
- NYSE (New York Stock Exchange): Oldest and largest, lists blue-chip companies.
- NASDAQ: Technology-focused exchange. Home to Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon.
- S&P 500: Index of 500 largest US companies, widely used as market benchmark.
- Dow Jones Industrial Average (DJIA): Price-weighted index of 30 major US companies.

Section 2: Key Financial Metrics

When evaluating a stock, investors look at:

1. Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
   - P/E = Share Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS)
   - A high P/E suggests investors expect high growth.
   - A low P/E may indicate undervaluation or poor growth prospects.
   - Average S&P 500 P/E historically: 15-25x.

2. Market Capitalization Categories
   - Large-cap: >$10 billion (e.g., Apple ~$3 trillion)
   - Mid-cap: $2B – $10B
   - Small-cap: $300M – $2B
   - Micro-cap: <$300M

3. Earnings Per Share (EPS)
   - EPS = Net Income / Number of Shares Outstanding
   - Higher EPS generally indicates better profitability.

4. Dividend Yield
   - Annual Dividend / Share Price × 100
   - Shows how much cash a company returns to shareholders.

5. 52-Week High/Low
   - The highest and lowest price of a stock over the past year.
   - Useful for identifying support/resistance levels.

Section 3: Investment Strategies

Growth Investing:
- Focuses on companies with strong earnings growth potential.
- Example companies: Tesla (TSLA), NVIDIA (NVDA), Amazon (AMZN).
- Higher risk, higher potential reward.

Value Investing:
- Seeks undervalued stocks trading below their intrinsic value.
- Made famous by Warren Buffett and Benjamin Graham.
- Example: Finding a stock with P/E of 10 when industry average is 20.

Index Investing:
- Investing in broad market indices rather than individual stocks.
- Lower fees, historically matches or beats most active managers.
- Popular index funds: Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO), SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY).

Dividend Investing:
- Focuses on companies with consistent dividend payments.
- Provides steady income stream.
- Examples: Johnson & Johnson (JNJ), Coca-Cola (KO), Procter & Gamble (PG).

Section 4: Technology Stocks Overview

The technology sector is one of the largest and most influential:

Apple (AAPL):
- World's most valuable company.
- Products: iPhone, Mac, iPad, Apple Watch, AirPods.
- Services revenue growing: App Store, Apple Music, iCloud.
- Ticker: AAPL on NASDAQ.

Microsoft (MSFT):
- Second most valuable company globally.
- Products: Windows, Office 365, Azure cloud, LinkedIn, Xbox.
- Heavy investment in AI (OpenAI partnership, Copilot).
- Ticker: MSFT on NASDAQ.

Alphabet / Google (GOOGL):
- Dominates online advertising (~90% search market share).
- Products: Google Search, YouTube, Android, Google Cloud.
- Ticker: GOOGL on NASDAQ.

NVIDIA (NVDA):
- Leading manufacturer of GPUs.
- Massive growth driven by AI/ML demand for H100 chips.
- Data center revenue surpassed gaming revenue.
- Ticker: NVDA on NASDAQ.

Section 5: Risk Management

- Diversification: Don't put all eggs in one basket. Spread across sectors.
- Dollar-Cost Averaging: Invest fixed amounts regularly regardless of price.
- Stop-Loss Orders: Automatically sell if stock drops below a set price.
- Emergency Fund: Keep 3-6 months of expenses in cash before investing.
- Position Sizing: No single stock should be more than 5-10% of portfolio.

Section 6: Understanding Stock Data

When looking at a stock quote, you'll typically see:

- Current Price: The last traded price.
- Open: Price at market open (9:30 AM ET on weekdays).
- Close/Previous Close: Price at market close (4:00 PM ET).
- Day High/Low: The highest and lowest prices during today's session.
- Volume: Number of shares traded today.
- Average Volume: Typical daily trading volume over 30/90 days.
- 52-Week High/Low: Highest and lowest price over the past year.
- Market Cap: Total company value at current price.
- Dividend Yield: Annual dividends as % of share price.

Markets are CLOSED on weekends and US federal holidays.
After-hours trading (4 PM – 8 PM ET) has lower volume and wider spreads.
